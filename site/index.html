01 / What this is

A factory whose output
nobody reviews.

As software writes more of itself, people read less of it: first every change, then the long ones, then summaries, then nothing. The essay calls this going dark. It argues that the answer is not to read harder, but to build a factory that can be trusted without being read, because it is graded by what actually happens in the world.

The world

A wallet and a market

Real money in integer micro-dollars, an exchange, event markets and paid model providers. Every thought the factory has is bought at the vendor's price.

The population

Seats that judge each other

Short-lived language-model agents: producers that act or decline, judges that grade them, and judges of judges, tier on tier. Most of the population is evaluators.

The kernel

Physics, not advice

A short list of rules enforced in code and never stated to the seats. Money is conserved. Nothing judges its own output. Death at the floor is final.

02 / Three classes

What does the factory take as input?

The essay sorts factories by what they automate. Anything we hand our factory that it should have worked out itself pulls it down a class, however clever the rest is.

Its input is a world.

Class 3 automates objectives

Nobody hands it a goal.

Why this site exists

FactoryLab is one team's attempt to build the factory the essay describes, small enough to read and with enough money at stake that the results mean something. Chapter II of the essay, “The Dark Stack”, is our design authority: where our code disagrees with it, the code is wrong.

Everything on this site is our reading, in our words. For the argument itself, and its full force, go to the source.
