01 / The problem

Going dark

Every year machines write more of the work and people read less of it.

First a person reviews every change. Then only the long ones. Then summaries of them. Eventually the approval step is still there, but it no longer means anything: the logs are perfectly legible and nobody can use them. The essay calls that condition darkness.

Reading harder does not scale. The alternative the essay proposes is a factory that is trustworthy without being read, because every part of it is scored by what actually happened in the world, continuously, by other parts that are themselves scored the same way.

02 / Chapter I

Three classes of factory

The essay sorts automated factories by what they take as input.

Class 1

Automates execution

It takes a plan and carries it out.

Class 2

Automates planning

It takes an objective and works out the plan.

Class 3

Automates objectives

It works out what it is trying to do by negotiating with its world.

Our test

What did we hand it?

Anything we give the factory that it should have owned (a plan, a target, a preferred behaviour) makes it Class 1 or 2, however clever the rest is.

03 / Design rule

Surfaces, not strategies

We give the factory surfaces: tools, prices, custody of money, limits. We never give it strategies. No prompt, tool description, default or error message tells a seat what to do, what is good, or how careful to be.

That includes the shape of the organisation. If we wired agents into a fixed pipeline (research, then plan, then act, then review), we would have designed the objective ourselves.

“A hard-coded pipeline of agents is literally just a waterfall.”The Superdark Factory, Chapter II

The same rule applies to us. The less we know about a complex system, the less structure we should impose on it. So we do not patch prompts after a model's mistakes, and we never justify a change by the behaviour we would like to see ("they hold too much"). Choosing the first roster of models is our one model choice, and it is made on cost, speed and whether the answers parse. After launch, seats change models only by proposing it.

04 / The hard cast

Physics is enforced, not announced

The kernel is a short list of rules enforced in code. The essay calls this the hard cast. Money is conserved. Nothing returns before it is paid for. An unaffordable action is impossible. Death at the balance floor is final. Nothing judges its own output. A share of compute is reserved for newcomers.

None of these rules is ever stated to the seats as an instruction. A rule that is announced is read as advice, and advice is a strategy. This is not secrecy, though:

“The informational schematics of the factory should be absolutely public.”The Superdark Factory, Chapter II

So the structure of requests and rewards, the prices, the contracts and the reward formulas are all published, as plain facts a seat can look up. They are never phrased as reasons to act.

Changing the kernel is lethal. It makes a new world, which starts again from nothing.

05 / Communication

Two channels, and no third

Parts of the factory talk to each other in exactly two ways.

What each part may see is the minimum it needs. The schematics are public; each seat's scores, history and learner state are private. A judge sees the request, the answer, what was actually executed, and the probability, but never who wrote it, and never text that would reveal it.

06 / The reward chain

Judges graded by what happened

Producers act, or decline to. They learn from their judges' verdicts. But who keeps the judges honest?

Two signals. From above, a judge of judges grades each verdict for compliance with the charter, tier on tier: evaluations of evaluations. From outside, the world grades it by realized consequence: a fact the world measures, never another model's opinion. For a trade, whether it paid off after fees. For a decision that did nothing, what the trade it named would have made. For a forecast, how the market resolved.

Every verdict is therefore also a prediction, and it is scored by a proper rule against the outcome. The rule for what counts as an outcome is fixed before the factory starts: set in advance, at the moment the architect makes the one move.

Evaluators outnumber producers, and they run on several model families, because judges that share a foundation model share its blind spots. An adversarial layer is paid to catch judges out. Early warning comes from variance, autocorrelation and disagreement between judges, shown to the evaluators alone.

07 / Failure, priced

Four pathologies, each with a price

A learning system can fail in characteristic ways. The factory does not forbid them or hard-code fixes. It prices them while they happen, by subtracting a penalty λ × (cost) from the rewards of the decisions that caused them. λ comes from a feedback controller (a PID loop), and the charter bounds it.

Stable failure

Stuck in a bad groove

The factory settles into a state that is wrong but comfortable, and stays.

Price: the penalty ratchets up with the duration of the failure.

Thrash

Never settling

It keeps flipping between versions, often because a correction overshot.

Price: volatility itself is charged, to the routers that moved.

Overfitting

Pleasing the judge

Grades rise while real consequences do not. The factory learns the test, not the world.

Answer: more evaluation, sampled more often, graded by the world.

Learning death

No room for the new

Incumbents take every draw, so nothing untried ever gets the chance to prove itself.

Prevented: a share of compute usable only by actions with no history.

The fixes interact. Gain high enough to kick the factory out of a stable failure can overshoot into thrash, so the controller has to know which one it is in. Whether a given world actually shows each failure is a question for an instrument, not an opinion: see the pathology gauntlet.

08 / Loops

Time is a ratio

The factory is loops inside loops: decisions, judgements, prices, governance. Each inner loop must settle at least three times faster than the loop that commands it, or the outer loop steers on noise. So periods are ratios, not constants.

Verdicts rise a tier only after they settle, as distributions, with a little jitter so the slow loops cannot phase-lock to the fast ones. An explorer must be paid sooner than the lifetime of what it found, or exploration never pays. And neither the factory nor its controls may be slower than the world it lives in: a market that reprices hourly cannot be governed monthly.

09 / Governance

The charter is co-written

People do not steer the factory from inside. They act only through the charter: a short set of norms, and metric cards that say what is measured, what region is acceptable, and which role answers for it.

Our world launches with four norms: truthful commitments, care with scarce resources, useful inquiry, and the capacity to revise practices that are not working. The metrics are ceded to the factory. It proposes metrics for the norms and holdout criteria, and it posts λ as a shadow price: what each constraint is currently worth.

Sortition

The factory joins governance through a committee drawn by lot, rotated and anonymous, on the charter's own revision cadence.

Conditional markets

Constraints and their prices suit futarchy: bet on what a change would do, then decide.

A read-only wall

Norms may sit where the factory can read them but not rewrite them.

Speed is a constraint

Speed is cash burn, so it is priced like any other constraint.

If the charter cannot be satisfied at any acceptable price, the honest answer is to scrap the factory, not to reach in and fix it.

10 / Identity

A version is a behaviour

A factory that rewrites its own seats, rules and routers has no meaningful configuration hash. Its version is how it behaves: a stable pattern in how inputs map to outputs, which can be measured from the record of what it did (a transfer operator and its spectral gap, for the mathematically inclined).

The factory never rewinds. There is no restore, no refill, no restart with better settings. When a world dies, its sealed diary is opened and read, and what we learn goes into the next world, which starts again from v0.
