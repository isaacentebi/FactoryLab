## The six decisions

**1. Kill.** Built as written. `factorylab kill --world W --ledger L` takes the ledger writer lock, reopens the original world, calls `Termination.kill("explicit_kill:operator")` — the only authority that may publish `Terminated` and the only path that releases the seal — prints the release and exits 3; `deploy/factorylab.service` sets `RestartPreventExitStatus=3`, so the kill is final against `Restart=always`. It steers nothing: the parser accepts no argument but the world and its ledger, and `test_kill_takes_no_argument_that_could_steer_a_world` pins that. One gap, documented rather than hidden: because it takes the writer lock, the operator must stop the unit first, so a process that will not stop cannot be killed by this command — `systemctl stop` alone still leaves the world resumable and the diary sealed.

**2. Cadence.** Built for testnet, not yet for the edition the launch manifest is drafted from. `worlds/testnet.toml` carries `tick_interval = "120s"` and `evaluation.consequence_backstop_events = 60`: two hours for a trade to show its result, and `min_ratio` of those, six hours, between charter activations, pinned by `test_testnet_governance_can_act_every_six_hours_of_world_time`. The horizon bounds only when a trade is judged; the population may still hold for any length and may vote its own tick. The world block now publishes the next activation boundary. Two gaps. `worlds/edition1-example.toml` still carries `tick_interval = "60s"` and no `consequence_backstop_events`, so it falls back to the 200-event default: the decided cadence is absent from the file the launch charter is drafted from. And "the rehearsal world runs shorter" is not in `worlds/`: the two scripted worlds run a 1 s tick and a 20-event backstop, which is the demonstration, and no short-tick testnet rehearsal manifest ships.

**3. Judges answer for verdicts.** Built as written, and it is the strongest piece of work in the round. A verdict is settled as a prediction that the judged return will not be blamed by the charter: when the window closes, the pricing pass's attributed blame share is the outcome, the Brier against the pre-outcome base rate enters standing beside payoff skill (`verdict.consequence`), the meta that conformed is graded against it too, and an antagonist is paid for exposing a high verdict that landed on a blamed return. Seat 2's exact strategy — `verdict=1, payoff=0` on a lazy producer — now costs `eval-a` 0.75 of verdict skill and drops its standing weight to 0.25, while the producer's own reward stays the verdict, unchanged. The care is in the edges: a window that never closed, or whose attribution evidence was released before it could be read, is closed out unscored rather than at one, so a missing fact is never performance; and a boundary amendment cannot unblame a verdict settling later, because a closed window keeps the cards and prices of its edition.

**4. Every vote is liable.** Built as written. Amendment, connector and retirement proposals are all refused before the reservation is spent unless they carry a `predicted_effect` naming a card, a direction and a horizon; `policy.promised` freezes that card's selector, observation version and acceptable region, measures a baseline at activation, and settles at the horizon on whether the measured value satisfied the frozen region — not on the proposer's direction, which was seat 2's complaint. Yes votes predict compliance, no votes its negation, and the score is a Brier on the region. Abstentions, failed proposals and missing evidence are censored. No gap I can find against the decision as written.

**5. New kinds of work.** All three parts built. A card's `answers_for` may name any registered emitted kind and that kind is measured in its own scope rather than as a producer; forecast predicates register like observations, as jailed `resolve(facts) -> bool`, preflighted against a closed window, versioned per id, and resolved only over facts that followed the sealed claim; and a registration declares which one of `judged`, `forecast`, `conformity`, `exposure` pays its kind, defaulting to `judged`, fixed after registration, with the seed kinds' shapes unredefinable. There is no fifth shape, and the disclosure block says what the four are without explaining why — the kernel enforces and does not announce. Gap: none against the decision; the remaining architect's hand is that the four shapes are still the architect's four.

**6. See the world.** All six parts built: a proposal may carry a `preflight_path` and admission judges "answered within the bounds" rather than a 2xx root; venue public reads reach any coin the venue lists while trading still refuses an unregistered market; `exchange.coins` and `venue.spot_pairs` are the launch seed and a `market` registration adds a pair the venue lists, reaching inventory and lots across resume; a connector may pay for data through x402 with an exact per-call cap from the same wallet, journaled and never resubmitted on replay; a metered public notebook (`note.put`/`note.get`) charges rent per byte-window, keeps its text when rent is unaffordable rather than being escapable by overwriting, and shows the wake only counts; and window facts carry market, funding, wallet and tick series bounded at 1,024 samples. The perimeter that was to stay, stayed: no network in the jail, every read through a journaled rail, every byte paid for.

## Is the intended functionality there, and is it the factory the essay describes?

The intended functionality is there, with one measured exception and one unrun proof. Every blocker the six seats reproduced is fixed behind a test that was red and is green here: a handle no longer collects its own round trip twice, a ballot cannot write to the venue, a registered observation now travels registration → proposal → vote → activation → priced settlement, the testnet manifest's two dead values are real, a class transfer confirms on a hash and an execution window, the demonstration reaches two governance boundaries inside the README's own 500 events, a parent can no longer buy a judge or a subject, and a death inside the jail no longer wedges the world. The exception is T47: the launch roster's 84% well-formed rate was answered with a token-budget edit that nothing tests and nothing has re-measured, and it is the one card the factory is known to fail from its first window. The unrun proof is the live rehearsal: the world is proved launchable against a recording, not against the venue.

Is it the factory the essay describes? Closer than any previous round, and on the two counts the essay cares most about it has moved from paper to code. The factory can now install what it proposes — a measurement it wrote can price a role, a kind it registered can be judged, forecast and paid in its own terms, a market it names can be traded, a source whose root 404s can be voted in and paid for — and the sensory organ is answerable: a judge's endorsement is settled against the charter's own blame, every vote carries a promised effect it is scored on, and no seat sits on its own retirement. What remains the architect's is narrower and more honest than before, and it is still real: the four reward shapes, the four norms, the seed cards of a charter that has not yet been re-drafted by the launch roster, and the perimeter of the jail. The essay's test is the wake rather than the design, and the wake has not been taken: no world has run on this main against a live venue, so what I can certify is that the mechanisms are present, tested and consistent, and that the only remaining reasons to doubt it are one unmeasured card and one unrun rehearsal — neither of them structural, both of them cheap, and both of them prerequisites to calling this a Class 3 factory rather than a credible attempt at one.
