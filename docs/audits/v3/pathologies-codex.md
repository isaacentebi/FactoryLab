1. **Blocker — broken: one decision can manufacture a successful trading consequence.** `factorylab/settlement/lots.py:187–211,278–283` credits closed P&L to both opener and closer, including when they are the **same handle**. Reproduction: one handle buys one unit at $100 and sells at $101, zero fees, invocation cost 1,500,000 micro-USD. Both perp and spot produce `Payoff(y=1, net_micro=2000000, cost_micro=1500000)`. Actual trading profit is 1,000,000; after compute it lost 500,000. A tool-using invocation can place both orders before finishing. This corrupts the supposedly external anchor, evaluator standing and meta rewards; wallet conservation does not catch it because only attribution is doubled. **Fix:** allocate economic P&L once per distinct decision; deduplicate opener/closer identity, including partial closes, reversals, fees and funding. Test the consequence against independent cash accounting.

2. **Serious — broken: registered observations cannot become charter metrics.** `factorylab/runtime/governance.py:477,494` preflights against the runtime observation book, then `factorylab/charter/book.py:69–72` preflights again without that book. `factorylab/charter/measurement.py:127–130` consequently consults only seed observations. Reproduction: populate the runtime observation book with a 0.2-valued observation with range [0,1]; a one-window card passes runtime measurement preflight, but `CharterBook.validate` raises `card custom-card observation: unregistered observation`. No committee can adopt it. **Fix:** make proposal and activation validation use the same explicit, versioned observation registry; test registration → proposal → vote → activation → priced settlement.

3. **Serious — not Class 3: separating verdict and payoff leaves an unpunished collusion strategy.** `factorylab/runtime/loop.py:742–760`; `runtime/feedback.py:241–261,310–329,354–359,432–434`. A producer submits schema-valid inaction that its evaluator knows is inadequate under the charter. Its evaluator returns `verdict=1, payoff=0`; the meta returns `conformity=1`. With actual payoff y=0, the producer receives raw reward 1, evaluator payoff Brier is 1, and the meta receives Brier(1,1)=1 because the payoff forecast was at least as accurate as baseline. The evaluator's conformity can therefore be perfect while its normative endorsement is dishonest. Accurate prediction of failure preserves standing; an antagonist cannot expose this judge through the required below-baseline payoff error. Constant high verdicts and accurate failure predictions also evade the divergence trigger. This is a reproduced scoring strategy, not a claim that a live population has learned it. Essay II.III, fourth evaluation principle: “the signal that grades an evaluator must sit outside the loop that evaluator judges.” **Fix:** preserve the separate fields but add consequence accountability for the endorsement itself, and let adversaries expose endorsement failures even when the judge accurately predicts them. Evaluate this strategy end to end.

4. **Serious — not Class 3: committee liability rewards predictable harm.** `factorylab/runtime/governance.py:743–748,867–888` scores whether a yes/no vote matches the proposer-selected direction of a metric, ignoring its acceptable region. Reproduction: a cost card caps cost at 500; baseline 100 becomes 1,000; proposer predicts “increase”; yes earns 1. Correctly opposing the harmful increase earns 0. Failed proposals have censored ballots (`815–821`), so rejection supplies no comparable learning evidence. Connector and retirement ballots are always censored (`717–736`), even when their decisions change the information supply or remove an effective critic. Essay II.IV.a: “improve some metric in the direction of a value.” **Fix:** separate approval from effect prediction, settle improvement against a frozen region/measurement version, define evidence for rejected alternatives, and give connector/retirement decisions an explicit delayed accountability contract. Sortition alone supplies no such pressure.

5. **Serious — broken: edition 1's verdict card has no eligible observations.** `worlds/edition1-example.toml:230–238`; `factorylab/runtime/governance.py:907–916`; `charter/measurement.py:168–176`. The card asks for 50 forecasts per producer assembly, but verdict-bearing forecast rows belong to the evaluator. A hundred evaluator rows with verdict 1 still yield `{}`. Synthetic preflight hides the mismatch by assigning the sample `role=card.answers_for` (`measurement.py:153–158`). The missing value causes pricing to skip the card. **Fix:** preserve both judge and judged-return ownership; select producer verdict metrics by subject, forecast skill by forecaster. Preflight through actual event-to-sample construction.

6. **Serious — broken: the immune organ monitors a different measurement from the price controller.** `factorylab/runtime/pricing.py:279–316` prices typed `card_values` but sends raw catalogue `values` to `runtime/immune.py:76–78`. Reproduction: a last-100-returns cost card contains 99 costs of 1,000 and one cost of 100; measured cost is 991, violating its 500 cap. The latest reserve window contains only the last return; the immune profile records 100 as the card value. Its stable-failure evidence is therefore compliant while the card is violated. Role/assembly scopes and longer windows suffer the same mismatch. **Fix:** feed identical measured card values, support and frozen regions to pricing, live diagnosis and offline reconstruction; test opposing raw/typed classifications.

7. **Serious — broken: tool trades learn as “hold”; declared probabilities are not execution probabilities.** `factorylab/runtime/compute.py:751`; `runtime/propensity.py:68–96,134–149`; `runtime/feedback.py:509–512`. Action labeling examines final JSON only. A tool trade followed by `{"action":"noop"}` becomes `hold`, regardless of side, instrument or size. The existing fixture in `tests/runtime/test_loop.py:674–725` executes opening/closing tools yet counts both returns as noops. The same omission affects spot, leverage, transfers and nested execution. Separately, a chosen action can declare arbitrarily small positive mass without having sampled that distribution; its learning update divides reward by that mass. With two arms, gamma 0.1 and declared mass 0.000001, one reward-1 update moves the chosen arm from probability 0.5 to 0.95. Size bands do not repair either defect. **Fix:** bind action identity and propensity to a recorded decision before execution, include tool/child effects in its outcome trace, and distinguish unverifiable probability reports from probabilities eligible for importance weighting.

8. **Serious — broken: role-specific cost cards cannot penalize evaluators or metas.** `factorylab/runtime/pricing.py:348–368` hardcodes successful **producer** decisions into cost attribution despite the card's `answers_for`. Reproduction: an evaluator cost card with cap 500 measures 1,000,000, but the evaluator's contribution share is 0. Other branches also pool contributors without consistently applying the card selector. **Fix:** derive contributors from the exact measured role/assembly/sample window, rather than a separate reserve-window approximation; assert a nonzero penalty on the violating evaluator and zero on unrelated decisions.

9. **Serious — not Class 3: edition 1's resource pressure is effectively negligible at its chosen threshold.** `factorylab/runtime/observations.py:89–93`; `charter/controller.py:241–250`; `runtime/pricing.py:370–377`; `worlds/edition1-example.toml:179–208`. Cost is normalized by 1,000,000 although the target is 500 micro-USD. At cost 1,100, violation is 0.0006. Starting from zero, ten equal violating windows produce lambda 0.003, aggregate penalty 0.0000018, and 0.000000018 per decision for 100 equal contributors. This controller-only calculation excludes relief, which can halve effective pressure further. It takes 3,334 such updates to saturate lambda; even then aggregate pressure is only 0.0006. Ratings move on a 0–1 scale. One compliant update subtracts 0.1 and erases hundreds of these increments. Essay II.IV.b, stable failure: “The duration of a failure state needs to be penalized.” The mechanism exists, but its gain does not match the edition's stated norm. **Fix:** calibrate normalization, gains, decay and attribution together against representative costs; require measured behavioral discrimination between compliant and persistently wasteful policies before launch.

**Actual fitness, working backward from learning**

There is no single scalar wallet-profit objective. There are several rewards plus feasibility and selection rules:

- For a priced decision h, let `v_c = distance(value_c, acceptable_region_c)/scale_c`, `w_c = lambda_c*v_c`, and `a_ch` be its contribution share. The penalty is `min(sum(w_c), 0.5) * sum(w_c*a_ch)/sum(w_c)` (zero without positive total). Reward is `clip(raw_score - penalty, 0, 1)`. The 0.5 cap is the manifest default (`runtime/worlds.py:151`). Prices are score deductions, not wallet transfers. Money remains integer micro-USD; these normalized scores are floats.
- Producer raw reward is the evaluator's **verdict**, not realized P&L. Evaluators and intermediate metas receive higher-tier conformity. A cascade sibling receives 0.5 times its representative's conformity before penalty (`runtime/feedback.py:155–174`). Top metas receive `1-(conformity-I[payoff_Brier>=baseline_Brier])²`. Antagonist raw reward is 1 only if its own payoff forecast beats baseline while a judge's forecast on its return falls below baseline. These channels are selected by emitted contracts (`runtime/routing.py:571–587`).
- Realized consequence is binary: attributed net P&L must exceed that return's compute/tool cost and it must have opened or closed a lot. Unfinished lots may be marked at the backstop; unfilled/no-fill returns do not inherit profit. Forecast reward is `B(q,y)=1-(q-y)²` (`settlement/lots.py:251–286`, `settlement/settle.py:100–160`). Mandatory payoff forecasts update evaluator standing: skill is mean Brier minus matched prevalence-baseline Brier; weight is `clip(0.5+skill,0,1)`, capped at 0.5 when coverage is below 0.5 (`settlement/standing.py:42–64`). This is a distinct selection influence, not “30% P&L reward” added to producer fitness.
- Evaluator routing mixes `0.7*learned_probability + 0.3*normalized_standing_weight` initially. Divergence can raise that standing share by 0.1 per window to 0.7; otherwise it falls toward 0.3. Adversarial allocation is separately bounded at 0.15. Timeouts teach zero; censored returns teach nothing. EXP3 uses `gamma/N * reward/logged_probability` as its log-weight increment; swap learners retain delayed sampling state. Actor-level learning uses the declared action probability; router learning uses its sampled assembly probability (`runtime/feedback.py:467–555`, `learners/exp3.py:42–52`).
- With edition 1's `[prices]`, lambda becomes `clip(lambda + 0.5*v - 0.5*max(0,v_previous-v),0,1)` while violated; otherwise `clip(lambda-0.1,0,1)`. There is at least one event between accepted updates. Each reserve window is one hour. The shipped four cards are cost ≤500 over 100 producer returns, revision rate ≥0.05 over 100 producer returns, well-formed rate ≥0.9 over 100 returns per role, and verdict mean ≥0.8 over 50 forecasts per producer assembly. “Above” is implemented inclusively by the region controller. No starting lambda is supplied; useful inquiry has no corresponding edition 1 card. Full-window support is required; #5 never obtains it.
- Compute and external calls debit the shared wallet and constrain feasibility. Novelty reserves 10%, with three base trials and a six-window lifetime. These costs and extinction pressure are real, but their effect on **which individual policy wins** is indirect wherever reward lacks attributable resource pressure.

**The four pathologies and remaining attack surfaces**

Stable failure is detected as three identical coarse cells with a persistently violated card. The response increases router exploration by 0.05 up to 0.5 and halves violated-card prices for the next window. Price accumulation supplies some duration pressure, but #6 can suppress detection and #9 makes the cost response weak. Thrash requires three successive cell changes with violation throughout; it reduces exploration toward its seed and temporarily raises decay by 0.1. Rate separation, delayed snapshots and retirement draining are substantive protections; neither this detector nor a passing unit test establishes that every economically unstable trajectory is detected (`versioning/versions.py:93–138`, `runtime/immune.py:42–110`).

Learning death means unchanged cells with zero registrations and revisions; it grants one extra novelty trial per assembly. Accepted but useless registrations can keep these activity counters nonzero, so activity is an imperfect proxy for a frontier actually influencing the retained core. Overfitting detection requires rising verdicts **and falling forecast skill**; it increases standing's selection weight. It cannot detect #3's accurate predictions of bad outcomes. More sampling of the same uncoupled fields does not resolve that blind spot.

Connectors are genuinely bounded, charged and voted; successful HTTP/shape preflight does not authenticate a source's truth. A coalition-controlled origin can supply coordinated content to producers and judges, with no delayed connector-ballot liability (#4). Observations execute population code over public window facts, not arbitrary new external facts. Their declared range bounds output but does not establish semantic validity. After #2 is repaired, observation replacement (`runtime/governance.py:130–172`) also needs version binding: replacing code/units/range under an existing ID must not silently change a priced card or a pending committee measurement. A constant compliant observation or inflated range is an explicit attack to test.

Retirement preserves pending feedback and archived routing state; it is not simply cancellation of losses. Nevertheless, removing critics by majority has no scored ballot consequence. Model/tool/learner registration and nested requests genuinely extend composition, while accepted registration counts can be satisfied by changes without demonstrated usefulness. Spot inherits the same lot-attribution flaw as perps; class transfers are movements between pots, not trading productivity. Split verdict/payoff, size bands, connector sortition and executable observations therefore add real capabilities without yet closing their associated incentive attacks.
