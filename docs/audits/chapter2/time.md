# Time and Phase audit: Chapter II §IV.b–c against FactoryLab

Auditor slice: *The Superdark Factory* Chapter II, §IV.b "Time and Phase" and §IV.c "Clocking the Factory" (ch2.md lines 277–322).
Code: `fast-loop-harness` @ 5049b0f. The audit was read-only. Probes ran offline only (sockets blocked, no keys read). Probe output is in `scratchpad/audit/runs-time/`.

## Verdict in one paragraph

The factory has **one ratio check, and it is applied to the wrong pair of loops**: `tick ≤ window / 3` (`runtime/worlds.py:531, 968`). Every other period is an unrelated absolute constant, and the constants sit in **four clock domains**:

- wall-clock nanoseconds (the window);
- declared-tick nanoseconds with a ×4 slack (decision deadlines);
- ticks consumed (horizons, backstop, cadence);
- internal event counts (forecast horizons).

A single wall-clock window (`novelty.window`, now `"1m"`) clocks about twelve outer loops. They all fire in the same instant, without jitter. Among them are the price PID, immune gain, stable-failure ratchet, sampling actuator, novelty share and grant, charter activation, policy grading, rent and treasury caps. Most of these outer loops run **faster** than the inner loops they correct. The recent edit that moved the window from 1h to 1m made this worse: it was made so that a test run would show the organs firing, and it silently re-clocked every one of those loops 60×.

The ten-second tick is a fiction. The last wall-clock edition-5 run delivered p50 18.1 s and mean 22.5 s. At that rate the 3:1 check fails on measured time, and 37% of decisions hit a wall-clock deadline. There, the learner is either paid the arm's long-run average or, for holds, **paid nothing at all**. That is Chapter II's learning death, produced by the clock. The prescribed pieces that do exist should be kept:

- `GovernanceCadence` (3× the measured slowest period);
- the cascade gate's jittered, completed-evidence window;
- the NOOP credit deferred by measured router latency;
- the successor chain for retired routers;
- mark-to-market at the backstop (anticipatory settlement).

## Answers to the key questions

1. **Coherent ratios or absolute constants?** Mostly absolute constants. Only three things are ratios:
   - `max_tick = window / min_ratio`, which applies the ratio to the wrong pair of loops (T2);
   - `GovernanceCadence` = 3 × slowest period;
   - the cascade gate = 3–4 × declared tick, measured against the tick rather than the inner loop it gates (T10).

   Everything else is an integer in one of four units (T3).
2. **Where do loop periods exceed the lifetime of what they score?** Five places:
   - the price loop re-measures configurations that have already changed, with about 90% sample overlap window to window (T2);
   - novelty patience (36 ticks, or 16 at the delivered rate) is shorter than the consequence that pays it (backstop 60), and the one-window grant is shorter than the grounded due (T5);
   - the capital loop (90–120 ticks) outlives the backstop and the trial lifetime (T13);
   - wall deadlines expire before tick-counted outcomes (T3, T4);
   - rehearsals (60–80 delivered ticks) end before the first governance activation (180) (T7).
3. **Is refactoring velocity bounded relative to feedback latency?** Only charter amendments and retirements are bounded, by the cadence. Registrations, router epochs, immune gamma, the PID and the sampling mix all move every window, and the novelty stock is re-granted every window (T6).
4. **Do delayed rewards reach the decision after the seat or router is retired or replaced?** Routers: yes. The successor chain, `routing.py:413-481` and `feedback.py:2124-2145`, should be KEPT. Seat inbox: yes, because outcomes are addressed to the handle (KEEP). Seat money: a late credit to a retired seat goes to the commons (`budget.py:187-199`), a deliberate R3-C choice; KEEP with the reason that retirement is cadence-gated. **But** rounds that cross a wall-clock deadline are paid the arm's own mean or are lost entirely (T4). `UpwardBuffer` releases reach nobody (T9).
5. **Is anticipatory settlement used everywhere it should be?** It is used for venue and event lots: marked at the backstop (`settlement/lots.py:13-18`, `runtime/polymarket.py:31-42`). KEEP. It is absent for non-market exploration (registrations, tools, observations, services) and for the capital loop (T18, T13). The provisional verdict is only a fallback when the world never speaks.
6. **1-minute pricing window, 15-minute completion timeout, 10-second ticks: are they coherent?** No (T3, T8):
   - The 10 s tick is at the clock floor and below the delivered 18–22 s.
   - The 1m window is 6 declared ticks but about 2.7 delivered ticks, so the 3:1 check passes only on paper (22.5 s > the 20 s cap).
   - The 900 s timeout was justified as "longer than a ten-minute tick" (`x402.py:34-35`), so the intended ratio was 1.5×. It is now 90 declared ticks, 15 windows and 1.5 backstops. One stalled socket freezes the single-threaded loop for 15 windows' worth of wall time. The window counter then advances by one, while every wall deadline expires at once.

## Findings

### T1 — CONTRADICTS — HIGH — one unjittered wall-clock window clocks about twelve outer loops at the same instant

- **Where:** `factorylab/runtime/pricing.py:225-257` (`_manage_reserve_window`: `_close_price_window` → `reserve.open_window` → `_issue_novelty_grant` → `charge_window` ×2 → `_activate_charter_if_due` → `_derive_regions`); `pricing.py:471` (immune `close_window`); `loop.py:392-397` (`treasury.open_window`, `_commons_check`, `_sampling_actuator` on the same boundary); `compute.py:998-1001` (connector cap); `polymarket.py:401-404` (order cap); `routing.py:515-530` (trial lifetime, grant); `world/treasury.py:207-213, 959-967` (Venice cap, forward wait).
- **ch2:** "this temporal deferral should also be diversified (jittered) to intentionally obfuscate entrainment"
- **Evidence:** every one of these loops shares `novelty.window_ns` and the boundary instant. The code orders them by hand inside the boundary ("frozen before the immune organ runs", `pricing.py:458-470`), which is the symptom: the price controller, the immune organ, the sampling actuator and the charter all act within one event on one another's unfinished output. Loops on one beam cannot separate bands. `a60b825` changed the window from 1h to 1m to make prices move. That one edit also changed:
  - novelty patience from 6 h to 6 min;
  - the grant from 1 h to 1 min;
  - the treasury strand wait from 2 h to 2 min;
  - the Venice rate cap from $10/h to $10/min;
  - connector and order caps to 60× looser;
  - rent charging to 60× more frequent.

  The commit message mentions none of these.
- **Action:** FIX. Give each outer loop its own period, derived as ≥ `min_ratio` × the measured period of the loop it commands (T2). Draw each period with its own jitter, and do not share one boundary. Money-safety caps (Venice, forward wait) move to explicit durations or tick counts that come from the rail's latency, not from the pricing window. The treasury's `open_window(index)` should stop reading `stats.reserve_windows`.

### T2 — CONTRADICTS — HIGH — outer loops run faster than the inner loops they correct; the 3:1 rule is checked only between tick and window

- **Where:** `runtime/worlds.py:529-531, 968-970` (the only ratio check); `pricing.py:444-452` (PID observes every window); `charter/controller.py:341-363` (`min_window_events` counts *internal events*, which always advance); `charter/measurement.py:409-470` (rolling last-n selection); `immune.py:196-206` (gamma step per window); `feedback.py:1557-1596` (sampling actuator per window); `charter/amendment.py:66,84` (`predicted_effect.window ≥ 1`); `governance.py:1584-1616`.
- **ch2:** "an inner loop must resolve itself several times faster than the outer loop"
- **Evidence:** the price PID closes every 6 ticks on a card whose sample is n = 10 settled forecasts per assembly. In the post-merge scripted run, about one new sample per assembly arrived per window, and window 9 had **zero** new settlements yet still produced a `price.update` (`runs-time/price_staleness.txt`). Each sample is therefore integrated about 10 times by the I-term. The immune organ steps router gamma every window (6 ticks) on consequence profiles that settle 10–60 ticks after the decisions made under the old gamma. A committee's policy promise can be graded 1 window (6 ticks) after activation, against a backstop of 60 and a governance period of 180. That grades governance on the unfinished transients of the loop it commanded, which is iatrogenic thrash exactly. `TimingRegistry` registers the dependency graph needed to check this, but only `leaf → governance`; the `price:<card>` loops are registered as leaves that govern nothing (`controller.py:203-228`).
- **Action:** FIX.
  1. Validate at load time and enforce at runtime, for every outer→inner pair, that outer period ≥ `min_ratio` × the inner loop's measured period. Use the `TimingRegistry` graph for this: prices govern card samples, immune governs routers, policy grading governs the consequence loop.
  2. A price update requires at least one *new* settled sample in scope, not `min_window_events` internal events.
  3. Floor `predicted_effect.window` to cover `min_ratio` × the slowest period.

### T3 — PATHOLOGY (aliasing) — HIGH — four clock domains, and a harness that cannot see the difference between them

- **Where:** window in wall ns (`pricing.py:228`); deadlines in declared ns × slack (`routing.py:823-835`, `feedback.py:422`, `settlement/consequence.py:426`); horizons in ticks consumed (`worlds.py:350-362`, `grounded.py:144-149`, `cadence.py:345-357`); forecast due in internal events (`feedback.py:437`, `settlement/forecast.py:93-101`); validation on the declared tick (`worlds.py:968-970`); `scripts/fastloop.py:8-12` (virtual clock).
- **ch2:** "optimization against the measurements diverges from the norm in the intervals between them"
- **Evidence:**
  - **Declared versus delivered.** `work/population-e5a` (60 wall-clock ticks, edition-5 roster) delivered tick gaps of p50 18.1 s, mean 22.5 s, p90 45.1 s and max 66.4 s against a declared 10 s. The 3:1 cap is 20 s, so the manifest validates while the running world violates it.
  - **Timeouts.** In the same run, 51 of 138 decisions timed out at the 220 s wall deadline: judge rounds 25/57, antagonist 7/9, producer 16/51, fills 3/4.
  - **The harness.** The fast harness runs a virtual clock at exactly 10.0 s (`runs-time/fastloop_diaries.txt`) and shows 0–1 timeouts. It is the metric that sampled too slowly to see the norm: `a60b825` was validated only there ("60 ticks close 9 pricing windows").
  - **The code knows.** `feedback.py:446-450` says: "its deadline is wall time while its forecasts' horizons count events, so an outage or a stalled loop can expire it before they come due."
- **Action:** FIX.
  1. Use one domain: ticks consumed, with the measured interval used only to convert to wall time for display and for money rails.
  2. Deadlines become tick horizons plus a ratio slack. Delete the ×4.
  3. Forecast horizons count ticks, not `self.n`.
  4. Manifest validation, and a runtime check, use `max(measured, declared)` as `GovernanceCadence` already does (`cadence.py:257-270`, KEEP).
  5. `fastloop` replays delivered-gap distributions taken from real diaries.

### T4 — PATHOLOGY (learning death) — HIGH — rounds that cross a wall-clock deadline pay the arm's average, or nothing

- **Where:** `runtime/feedback.py:1997-2059` (`_learn_router_return`); `learners/base.py:74-79` (`ObservedRewards.neutral` = the arm's observed mean); `feedback.py:1688-1721` (`_settle_opportunity_cost`); `grounded.py:13-16`.
- **ch2:** "A population compensated only for long-run averages ceases to produce variation"
- **Evidence:**
  - **(a)** A timed-out round is credited `target.observed.neutral(prop.chosen)`, the arm's own long-run mean. The later real score "trains no learner a second time". At 37% timeouts (T3), more than a third of rounds pay averages.
  - **(b) Lost round.** Take a producer hold whose decision hits the wall deadline while it is grounded-pending. It is not learned at the cutoff (`feedback.py:2027-2030`). When its opportunity-cost settlement arrives, it is refused as "learned once already, neutrally, at its cutoff" (`feedback.py:2038-2044`), because `"opportunity-cost-v1"` does not start with `GROUNDED_DEFINITION`. It trains nothing, ever. The offline probe `runs-time/probe_lost_round.py` prints `LOST ROUND`. In e5a, 12 of the 30 opportunity-cost settlements arrived after a timeout. At the delivered rate, the grounded due (10 ticks ≈ 225 s) always falls after the 220 s deadline, so in live runs **nearly every hold is unlearned**.
- **Action:** FIX.
  1. Immediately: treat `OPPORTUNITY_DEFINITION` as the grounded family. Better, key the exemption on `handle ∈ grounded contracts` rather than on a string prefix.
  2. Structurally: the learning cutoff is the decision's own horizon in ticks (T3). A cutoff credit, if one is kept, must not be the arm's own mean. Use the router's zero-consequence baseline, as the NOOP credit does.

### T5 — PATHOLOGY (learning death) — HIGH — novelty patience is shorter than the consequence that pays it

- **Where:** `routing.py:497-530` (`_unhistoried`, `_novelty_grant_open`); `pricing.py:260-268`; `worlds.py:378-382`; toml `[novelty] max_lifetime_windows = 6, window = "1m"`.
- **ch2:** "some share of the exploratory population is allowed to live longer than justified"
- **Evidence:** the trial lifetime is 6 windows = 36 declared ticks, about 16 delivered ticks. The consequence backstop is 60 ticks and the grounded close is 30 ticks. A novel assembly's protection therefore ends before its first marked consequence can arrive, even at the declared rate. At the delivered rate it ends before grounded close. The learning-death grant lives exactly one window (6 declared ticks, about 2.7 delivered) and is consumed only by a delivered trial beyond the base allowance. That makes it shorter than the grounded due (10 ticks), so the cure the immune organ issues for learning death cannot be collected. The scripted run flags `pathology.learning_death` 2–4 times in 120 ticks. Before `a60b825` these were 6 h and 1 h.
- **Action:** FIX. Express the trial lifetime and grant lifetime as multiples (≥ `min_ratio`) of the measured consequence period (max(backstop, p90 settlement)), not as window counts. `routing.py:497-507` already cites the rule; make the numbers obey it.

### T6 — PATHOLOGY (thrash) — MEDIUM — no speed limit on refactoring except charter and retirement

- **Where:** `governance.py:752-870` (registrations `_instantiate` then `_open_epoch` immediately); `routing.py:938-1004` (new router identity per shrinking epoch); `kernel/reserve.py:55-76` (novelty budget = share × *unlocked stock* per window); `immune.py:44-68`; `cadence.py` (gates only amendments and retirements, `governance.py:1245-1266, 1462-1478`).
- **ch2:** "some speed limit needs to be applied to the velocity with which the factory refactors"
- **Evidence:** configuration changes are rate-limited only by money. The novelty reserve re-grants 10% of the unlocked stock (about $12) **every window**, which is a stock paid out per period. Changing the window from 1h to 1m therefore raised the registration budget 60× per unit time: at `trial_amount` $0.05, that is up to about 240 registrations per minute, each re-epoching routers. The immune gamma, PID, decay and sampling mix also step every 6 ticks. The configurations they change are scored 10–60 ticks later. KEEP the one limit that is right: `GovernanceCadence` for charter and retirement.
- **Action:** FIX. Make the novelty share a flow per measured consequence period. Limit router epochs (and so registrations admitted into a routed kind) to at most one per inner-loop period, or gate them like amendments. Clock the organs per T2.

### T7 — MISSING — MEDIUM — no measurement of the viable governance band, and no alarm when it is empty

- **Where:** `runtime/cadence.py:243-255` (slowest = max(backstop, p90 forecast latency, oldest outstanding)); `scripts/rehearsal.py:62-67` ("duration": "10m"); `docs/architecture/capital-loop-rehearsal.md:165` (30m); `work/population-e5a/observer/report.json` (`first_activation_lower_bound_ticks 180`, `completed_ticks 60`).
- **ch2:** "has no viable governance tier" / "between an upper bound of sampling noise and a lower bound"
- **Evidence:**
  - **The measurement is inert.** The pooled latency sample is holds that resolve in 1–2 ticks (e5a and the scripted cadence settlements are all 1–2 ticks), so the backstop constant always wins and governance sits at 180 ticks by construction. This is not ch2's measurement ("inject a small, deliberate intent revision and measure ... settling time"). There is no spectral or settling-time identification.
  - **No upper bound.** Nothing checks the bound against lagging the world: world repricing rate against governance period.
  - **No alarm when there is no governance tier at all.** A 10-minute rehearsal (60 ticks) and a 30-minute capital loop (about 80 delivered ticks) both end before the first possible activation.
- **Action:** BUILD.
  1. A settling-time measurement: after each activation, measure time to redistribution of the per-card score series. This feeds `slowest_period`.
  2. A launch and runtime viability check: `min_ratio × slowest ≤ min(run length, world repricing period)`. Otherwise record `governance.nonviable` and, per ch2, accelerate lower loops or merge levels.

### T8 — PATHOLOGY (requisite velocity) — HIGH (live) — the factory reacts no faster than its slowest model call

- **Where:** `world/x402.py:31-37` (`MODEL_COMPLETION_TIMEOUT_S = 900`, "longer than a ten-minute tick"); `world/openrouter.py` (commit 84e7dfc); `runtime/loop.py:382-451` (single-threaded `_process_event`: watchers, fills, treasury and routing in one thread); `live.py:98-112` (late ticks, no catch-up).
- **ch2:** "the factory is expected to outrun the world"
- **Evidence:** all nine seats and all safety processing share one thread. A completion may block for 900 s, which is 90 declared ticks, 15 windows and 1.5 backstops, while `max_leverage = 3` perps sit open. Fills, liquidation warnings and watchers wait behind it. After the stall, `now_ns` jumps 15 min: every 220 s deadline expires in the same event (mass cutoffs, T4), the window index advances by one (not 15), and tick-counted horizons do not move. The timeout's stated ratio ("longer than a ten-minute tick", 1.5×) became 90× when the tick became 10 s. The previous unbounded wait (half an hour, per the commit) was worse, so the change was directionally right.
- **Action:** FIX. Take model I/O off the safety path (fills, watchers, reconcile and kill-adjacent wind-down on their own cadence). Set the completion deadline as a ratio of the delivered tick (≤ one outer-loop period). An expired call is a timed-out decision in tick terms.

### T9 — UNPRESCRIBED — MEDIUM — `UpwardBuffer`, the `leaf/governance` loops and the price-loop closures are a dead duplicate of the cascade gate

- **Where:** `kernel/timing.py:66-142` (`UpwardBuffer`), `10-51` (`TimingRegistry`); `runtime/bootstrap.py:278-283`; `runtime/feedback.py:1299-1312` (adds and releases; ledgers `upward.release`); `charter/controller.py:165, 203-228, 415-416`; `runtime/resume.py:660` (`_KERNEL_FIELDS` "timing", "buffer"); `runtime/summary.py:43`.
- **ch2:** "information is intentionally withheld from management until it settles"
- **Evidence:**
  - **Nothing reads the output.** `upward.release` is written and read by nobody. No governance, committee or meta consumes it. It was already flagged "`UpwardBuffer` dead" in `docs/audits/v2/triage.md:56` and never removed.
  - **Its parameters ignore the manifest.** It uses `jitter=1`, not the manifest's `jitter_fraction`.
  - **Its state grows forever.** Every consequence settlement and every price update appends a closure timestamp to `TimingRegistry`, and those lists grow without bound in every checkpoint. The only reader is `UpwardBuffer.release`.
  - **The prescribed mechanism lives elsewhere.** Ch2's queue ratio is actually implemented by `runtime/cascade.py` `CascadeGate`, which releases a distribution to the meta tier (KEEP).
- **Dependents and blast radius:** bootstrap construction; the feedback settlement loop; the `PriceController(timing=…)` parameter; checkpoint fields `timing`/`buffer` (a resume must ignore them in older snapshots); `stats.upward_releases`; `tests/kernel/test_timing.py`; `tests/charter/test_controller.py:180-205`. There is no manifest-hash impact.
- **Action:** DELETE `UpwardBuffer` and its ledger path. Keep `TimingRegistry` **only if** T2 adopts it as the outer→inner dependency graph and closure-period source (it is the only per-loop closure clock in the code). Otherwise delete it too.

### T10 — CONTRADICTS — MEDIUM — the cascade gate's 3:1 is measured against the declared tick, not the loop it gates
